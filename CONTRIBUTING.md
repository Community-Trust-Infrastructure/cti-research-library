# Contribute to Community Trust Infrastructure

**CTI is an open research architecture, not a finished standard.**

We invite researchers, community practitioners, developers, funders, policymakers and organizations to help challenge, test and advance the work.

Have you developed research, tools, case studies or practical approaches related to trust, community governance, verification, coordination or capital allocation? We welcome your contribution.

You can contribute by:

- Sharing relevant research and literature.
- Documenting real-world implementations and experiments.
- Challenging architectural assumptions or identifying gaps.
- Proposing new coordination or verification mechanisms.
- Sharing lessons from communities already doing this work.

Contributions do not need to endorse CTI. Critical perspectives, alternative approaches and evidence that challenges the architecture are equally valuable.

Our goal is to develop shared knowledge while respecting the autonomy, authorship and lived experience of the communities generating it.

**Explore the research. Challenge the assumptions. Contribute what you're learning.**

## Choose a contribution pathway

**Research library:** Suggest a publication, original source, literature note, methodological critique, citation correction or annotated case study. [Open a research submission issue](https://github.com/Community-Trust-Infrastructure/cti-research-library/issues/new?title=Research%20contribution%3A%20).

**Architecture and governance:** Suggest a change to an architectural assumption, design principle, validation approach, coordination mechanism or governance boundary. [Open an architecture discussion issue](https://github.com/Community-Trust-Infrastructure/cti-research-library/issues/new?title=Architecture%20discussion%3A%20). Architectural proposals are evaluated separately from literature submissions; inclusion in the library does not imply acceptance as an architectural claim.

**Experiments and field learning:** Share context, methods, observed outcomes, failure modes and limitations, including negative findings. [Open an experiment or case-study issue](https://github.com/Community-Trust-Infrastructure/cti-research-library/issues/new?title=Field%20learning%3A%20).

## Stewardship and review

1. Open an issue with a short description, relevant original sources, author or community attribution, permissions and any uncertainties.
2. Stewards review relevance, provenance, permission to share, community consent, privacy and whether the submission is research, an experiment or an architectural proposal.
3. Accepted material is added through a reviewed pull request or a steward-maintained change with appropriate attribution and a traceable edit history. Material does **not** enter the canonical research library automatically.
4. A contribution can be discussed, revised, deferred or declined; the reason should be communicated openly where safe and appropriate. A published research item does not establish consensus or endorsement.

**Contributor rights and safety:** Retain authorship and attribution. Only share material you have the right to share. Do not upload identifying or sensitive community records, participant images, private keys, wallet access information or unpublished field data. Remove or aggregate personal details and obtain meaningful community consent before sharing. No sensitive community information is required to participate. If a case cannot safely be described publicly, submit a high-level non-identifying description instead.

This repository has not yet adopted a general content or code license; contributors should state reuse permissions for their original material rather than assuming unrestricted reuse.
