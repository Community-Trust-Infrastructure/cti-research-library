## Research question

> Financial infrastructure is becoming programmable and interoperable. Can the evidence and operational trust upon which decisions depend become portable and interoperable too?

## Abstract — editorial synopsis

Community Trust Infrastructure (CTI) proposes an open, technology-agnostic reference architecture for capturing, interpreting, validating and carrying forward evidence of real-world activity as portable **trust context**. The research examines whether communities can make their existing coordination and contributions recognizable to institutions without repeating burdensome due diligence or surrendering local governance. CTI distinguishes cryptographic record integrity from meaning, evidentiary validity from governance authorization, and evidence/decision infrastructure from settlement and monetary systems.

This is a **proposed architecture**, not a deployed standard, objective trust score or proven mechanism for increasing funding. Sarreya provides practice-based observations and implementation failures; SportFi and Bolivia are prospective evaluation settings. Canada–EU monetary interoperability remains a separate exploratory branch.

## Canonical functional sequence

**Community activity → signals → attestations → validation → trust context → decision / policy interface → coordination or resource allocation**

This is a conceptual sequence, **not** a required software pipeline. Operational and financial execution remain outside CTI's evidence-and-decision interface.

## Key distinctions

| CTI distinguishes | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Record integrity / meaning | An unaltered record may still be misleading |
| Evidentiary validity / governance validity | True claims can be unauthorized; authorized claims can be wrong |
| Trust context / universal scoring | Receiving actors retain responsibility for contextual judgment |
| Human stewardship / automated execution | Technical capability does not confer legitimate authority |
| Capital allocation / settlement | Deciding what should move is distinct from executing a transfer |
| Research proposition / validated outcome | Sarreya and SportFi do not establish generalized CTI effectiveness |

## Evidence and publication status

The manuscript's publication note states that pilot observations are drawn from team-review drafts and should not be treated as independent audits. Its source register identifies internal AIFS–Sarreya materials and an earlier CTI working manuscript; the FundsChain comparison requires primary-source verification. Version 2.2 does **not** claim a completed literature review, external validation of pilot outcomes, measured reporting-cost savings or demonstrated funder-allocation changes.

For formal citation or quotation, consult the original PDF. The editorial summary above is not a substitute for the full manuscript.

## Invitation to critique

Constructive scholarly, technical, governance, policy and community-practice feedback is welcome. Especially useful contributions include primary-source corrections, challenges to the architectural assumptions, concrete failure modes, safeguarding considerations, comparable implementations, falsifiable tests and evidence that contradicts CTI's propositions. Contributions should not disclose sensitive participant data or unapproved partner details.
