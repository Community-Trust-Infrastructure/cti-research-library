# Donor-Retained Capital and Community-Directed Yield

**Status:** Exploratory research — not a validated CTI implementation.

## Research question

Can capital ownership, investment returns and community decision-making be separated in ways that preserve donor participation while increasing community agency?

## Hypothesis

Donors may be able to retain ownership of invested principal while making realized, distributable returns available for community-directed funding.

Community Trust Infrastructure could provide evidence, validation and governance context for accountable allocation decisions without managing investments, holding donor assets or determining investment strategies.

## Research priorities

- Compare donor-retained capital with conventional charitable endowments, donor-advised funds and investment-income donations.
- Investigate Canadian charitable receipting and legal requirements.
- Examine governance models for community-directed distributions.
- Evaluate investment risk, liquidity, costs and sustainability.
- Explore potential implementations using existing financial infrastructure.

## Public sport infrastructure stewardship — exploratory application

Infrastructure investment creates long-term stewardship obligations that are frequently separated from the original capital funding decision.

This research explores whether donor-retained capital and community-directed investment returns could help finance the ongoing maintenance, accessibility and programming of public sport infrastructure, while preserving municipal oversight and increasing transparency and community participation.

This is a proposed application, not an established financing model. Further investigation is needed into municipal responsibilities and liability, investment and liquidity risks, eligible uses of distributions, public accountability, and community decision-making authority.

## Potential reference cases

Bread Cooperative and SportFi offer relevant mechanisms and implementation experience for investigation. Neither should be treated as proof of the proposed donor-retained capital model.

## Invitation

We welcome research, legal perspectives, implementation examples and critical analysis that could help establish whether this model is feasible, beneficial and appropriate for community finance.
