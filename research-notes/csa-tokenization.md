## Central contribution
The Canadian Securities Administrators' tokenization work provides a regulatory frame for evaluating tokenized financial products and market infrastructure. The CTI question is what remains unresolved when ownership representations and settlement mechanisms become digitally programmable.

## Evidence and status
**Regulatory research / Phase 1 findings.** The CSA Collaboratory landing page is linked. Exact report pagination, quotations and individual findings should be checked against the published PDF before using this note in academic or regulatory submissions.

## Critical interpretation
A token may record a claim or enable transfer without automatically settling the legal questions of beneficial ownership, custody, enforceability, governance, investor protection or the reliability of external information. These are distinct institutional design problems.

## CTI architecture implications
- Keep **trust and decision context** distinct from financial execution and settlement.
- Require a clear connection between claims, accountable actors, validation procedures and legal rights.
- Design technology-neutral interfaces that can work with conventional and tokenized rails.
- Treat Canadian monetary sovereignty as an adjacent settlement consideration, not as something CTI itself can guarantee.

## Limitations and counterarguments
Regulated capital markets and grassroots community funding differ in rights, participant protections, transaction sizes and governance. Regulatory analysis identifies constraints and questions; it is not empirical validation of the CTI architecture.

## Questions for applied testing
1. What evidence must be established before an institution authorizes a transfer to a community?
2. How are contested or revoked claims handled after financial settlement?
3. Which assurance requirements can be proportionate to transaction risk without excluding small organizations?
