# World Bank FundsChain — institutional comparison

**Working research note · CTI Version 2.2 §24 · November 2026**  
**Source class:** Institutional case study / programmable development finance and verification infrastructure  
**Evidence status:** Public implementation claims and technical details **await verification against original World Bank documentation**.

## Institutional proposition

The World Bank Group's FundsChain initiative is a useful comparator for the relationship between transaction traceability, supporting-document verification, auditability and purpose-bound financial flows. CTI Version 2.2 refers to reported 2024–2026 implementation and evolution updates, including a reported Ethiopia pilot. This note does not independently establish the program's features or outcomes.

## The critical distinction

**FundsChain's relevant domain:** Tracking and controlling financial flows, transaction records and institutional compliance.

**CTI's research domain:** Whether claims about underlying real-world activity can be validated with legitimate, context-specific authority and carried between independently governed actors.

A cryptographically valid financial record does not establish that a community-defined activity or outcome occurred.

## Research questions

1. Can institutional financial workflows consume externally validated community activity attestations?
2. Can the originating community retain authority over what is recorded, validated, disclosed and contested?
3. How should governance provenance, revocation and confidence limitations travel with an evidence claim?
4. What does a purpose-bound transfer actually verify: the use of funds, the occurrence of activity, or its social value?
5. How can interoperable evidence avoid requiring communities to adopt an institution's governance model?

## Limits

No interoperability between FundsChain and CTI is claimed. FundsChain is **not** presented as a CTI reference implementation or proof that community-led evidence governance has been achieved. Source dates, reported Ethiopian deployment details and any claims of programmable enforcement need primary-source review.
