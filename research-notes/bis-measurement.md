## Central contribution
The CTI research register identifies this working paper as relevant to the difficulty of interpreting on-chain activity. A ledger can establish that recorded events occurred within a system; it does not, by itself, establish the economic or social meaning of those events.

## Critical interpretation
A transaction count can be technically accurate while misleading as a measure of genuine participation, social value or economic benefit. Classification, off-chain context and data provenance matter. Different operational definitions can produce different conclusions from the same records.

## CTI architecture implications
- Separate **signal capture** from **validation** and **decision authorization**.
- Store provenance, interpretation rules, confidence limits and correction mechanisms with the trust context.
- Avoid treating blockchain immutability as proof that an impact claim is true.

## Limitations and counterarguments
Transparent ledgers can still substantially improve auditability, reconciliation and detection of inconsistencies. The point is not that on-chain evidence lacks value, but that its evidential scope is bounded. CTI must also explain when added validation is worth its cost.

## Questions for applied testing
1. Which community activity signals can be independently checked without intrusive surveillance?
2. How often do two authorized stewards interpret the same signal differently?
3. What is the lowest-cost validation process sufficient for a given allocation decision?
