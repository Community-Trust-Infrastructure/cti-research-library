# Canton / Broadridge — institutional interoperability

**Source:** Canton Network and Broadridge Distributed Ledger Repo (DLR), institutional infrastructure references  
**Original / institutional source:** https://www.broadridge.com/  
**CTI editorial status:** Working analytical note · 10 November 2026 · Not peer reviewed

> This is a CTI interpretation of a research source or institutional precedent. It is not an endorsement by the source organization. Specific empirical claims are intentionally withheld where original-document verification is outstanding.

## Central contribution
Canton and Broadridge's distributed-ledger repo infrastructure are institutional reference points for coordinated financial workflows among separately governed participants. They are **not** direct evidence that community trust or impact validation has been solved.

## Evidence and status
**Institutional infrastructure and provider claims; primary technical documentation and independent assessment pending.** This note intentionally does not repeat unverified throughput or dollar-volume figures. Specific implementations, legal structures and interoperability guarantees require source-level review.

## Critical interpretation
Interoperability can allow institutions to coordinate without becoming one organization. Yet technical interoperability is not equivalent to shared meanings, legitimate authority or agreement about what constitutes adequate evidence.

## CTI architecture implications
- Define replaceable interfaces between evidence, validation, authorization and settlement.
- Preserve institutional and community autonomy over their own governance rules.
- Make the provenance of delegated authority legible across systems.
- Avoid requiring a particular ledger or vendor to interpret trust context.

## Limitations and counterarguments
Institutional finance benefits from formal legal agreements, regulated intermediaries, collateral standards and operational budgets that may not exist in grassroots settings. Transfer of design principles is plausible; transfer of institutional performance claims is not.

## Questions for applied testing
1. Can two independently governed communities exchange validated evidence without adopting identical rules?
2. Which claims must be standardized, and which can remain locally defined?
3. What is the cost of maintaining interoperability when one provider exits?
