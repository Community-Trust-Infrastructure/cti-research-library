# ENS DAO — delegation, execution and community authority

## Central contribution
ENS DAO is a governance reference for studying how a distributed community can define rules, authorize action and delegate operational execution. The key question for CTI is not whether governance is “on-chain,” but whether an actor's mandate is legitimate, bounded, auditable and revocable.

## Evidence and status
**Governance documentation; specific 2026 foundation/endowment proposals still need primary-source verification.** The present note does not assert that a particular proposal passed or that delegation outcomes were successful.

## Critical interpretation
A cryptographic signature establishes control of a key, not necessarily valid authorization by the affected community. The authority chain must specify who delegated power, for what purpose, for how long and with what appeal or revocation path.

## CTI architecture implications
- Model **governance provenance** alongside attestation provenance.
- Distinguish policy-making authority from bounded operational execution.
- Record scope, expiry, revocation and conflict-of-interest controls for validators and agents.
- Support contestation and corrections without making a universal reputation score.

## Limitations and counterarguments
Token-based voting can concentrate power, reward financial stake over lived experience and impose participation burdens. Community governance may require non-token methods, culturally appropriate deliberation and safeguards for vulnerable participants.

## Questions for applied testing
1. Can a community revoke a steward's mandate and have that change respected across systems?
2. Who can challenge an attestation when they lack a wallet or governance token?
3. How can delegation remain accountable without requiring every member to vote on operational details?
